HVAC Week in Review: August 11-17, 2026

Industry-wide price hikes hit equipment and parts, a Senate bill seeks to overhaul DOE efficiency standards, Samsung targets AI data center cooling, R-290 heat pumps reach multi-family scale, and refrigerant litigation intensifies. Here's the week in HVAC.

1. Industry-wide HVAC price increases take effect

A broad wave of manufacturer price increases took effect throughout August 2026, hitting equipment, parts, controls, valves, motors, and accessories. Most disclosed increases fall in the single digits, though some reach significantly higher: Legend Valve products up to 25%, Lau parts more than 16%, and several manufacturers — including Amana, Daikin, and Goodman — announcing increases up to 7% (ACHR News).

The repricing cycle began August 1 and extends through the end of the month, with additional increases from Emerson Nidec Motors (August 16) and Miller/Nortek Equipment (August 26) still ahead. For contractors and homeowners, this means equipment replacement costs are trending upward — another reason to prioritize maintenance and timely replacement decisions.

2. Energy Efficiency Reform Act introduced in Senate

On August 6, Sen. Mike Lee (R-Utah), chairman of the Senate Committee on Energy and Natural Resources, introduced the Energy Efficiency Reform Act of 2026. The legislation would reshape how the Department of Energy sets energy conservation standards for approximately 60 household and commercial products, including air conditioners and water heaters (Contracting Business).

Key provisions: the bill would eliminate EPCA's current mandatory six-year review requirement, require DOE to finalize its proposed Process Rule within 270 days, strengthen federal preemption of state standards, and require DOE to account for installation costs, venting, fuel types, and consumer costs when setting standards. The bill is supported by AHRI, ACCA, and the American Gas Association. It now moves to committee consideration.

Why it matters.If passed, this legislation would change the rhythm of HVAC efficiency standards — potentially slowing the pace of mandatory efficiency upgrades while giving manufacturers more predictability. For homeowners, the practical impact depends on whether DOE's Process Rule is finalized on time and how product classifications are revised.

3. Samsung expands HVAC manufacturing for AI data center cooling

Samsung opened a new HVAC manufacturing facility in Pune, India, on August 13, operated by its subsidiary FläktGroup. The 13,826-square-meter plant is planned to produce up to 6,500 HVAC units annually, targeting the fast-growing AI data centre cooling market across India and the Asia-Pacific region (Moneycontrol).

The move signals a broader trend: AI data centers are driving unprecedented demand for high-capacity precision cooling. AAON also reported record Q2 2026 sales driven in part by its BASX data center cooling business. As computing density increases, data center HVAC is becoming a distinct growth sector within the broader industry.

4. R-290 heat pumps reach multi-family residential scale

Italian manufacturer Rhoss launched new R-290 (propane) heat pump models for multi-family residential applications on August 11, extending its MidiPACK-PI range to heating capacities of 64.7 kW with operating ranges from -20°C to 40°C and water temperatures up to 80°C (PV Magazine). This follows Daikin's March 2026 launch of a complete R-290 range covering 17 to 2,000 kW.

The pattern is clear: European manufacturers are moving aggressively toward natural refrigerant heat pumps at commercial scale, solving the flammability challenge with monobloc designs that keep the entire refrigerant charge outdoors. U.S. adoption depends on safety code updates, but the product pipeline is maturing rapidly.

5. Refrigerant rule litigation intensifies

The legal battle over the EPA's May 2026 rule — which raised allowable GWP limits for supermarket refrigeration and remote condensing units from 150/300 to 1,400, delaying stricter thresholds until 2032 — continues to build. A coalition of 18 states plus Washington, D.C. and New York City filed a petition on July 21 in the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals, arguing the rule violates the AIM Act's one-year lead-time requirement and undermines the 85% HFC reduction target by 2036 (ClearBlue Markets). The NRDC filed a separate suit on July 20 (NRDC).

Industry groups — including HARDI, ACCA, AHRI, and PHCC — have filed similar challenges, arguing that allowing new commercial refrigeration systems to continue using high-GWP refrigerants while the AIM Act's allowance system phases down total HFC supply will artificially inflate demand and drive up refrigerant prices across the board, including in residential HVAC (Homepros). The EPA confirmed that twelve states — including New Jersey — have their own HFC restrictions in addition to federal rules (EPA).

The week's takeaway

Three threads connect this week's news. First, cost pressure: manufacturer price increases and refrigerant supply uncertainty are pushing equipment costs upward. Second, regulatory uncertainty: the Senate bill, the EPA rule rollback, and ongoing litigation all create a moving target for contractors and homeowners trying to plan equipment investments. Third, product innovation continues despite the uncertainty — R-290 heat pumps, AI data center cooling, and natural refrigerant adoption are all advancing.

For homeowners, the practical guidance remains the same: maintain what you have, replace proactively before failure, and work with a contractor who tracks these regulatory and product changes.
