When a Smart Thermostat Loses Support: What Still Works, What Stops, and How to Buy for the Long Haul

Your furnace may last 15 years. The app behind your smart thermostat may not. Here's what actually stops when a manufacturer ends support, why nearly 89% of smart products never say how long support lasts, and a checklist for buying controls that keep working.

A furnace or heat pump can run 15 years or more. The smart thermostat controlling it is really two products: a wall control, and a cloud service that runs on the manufacturer's servers. The wall control can last as long as the equipment. The cloud service lasts only as long as the manufacturer keeps paying for it. That gap matters to any homeowner choosing a thermostat, and to any contractor who installs one.

A real example: what happened in October 2025

The clearest recent case is the end of support for first- and second-generation Nest Learning Thermostats (2011 to 2014 models). We use it here because the manufacturer documented it in detail, not to single out one brand. Starting October 25, 2025, those units were unpaired from the app. Owners lost remote control, notifications, phone-based settings, third-party assistants and connected features like Home/Away Assist (Google Home Help). Linked smoke and CO alarms also disconnected, so the emergency shut-off that turned off the HVAC equipment in a fire alarm no longer runs (Google Home Help).

What kept working: control at the thermostat itself, the pre-set schedule, and switching between temperature modes. The devices also stopped receiving software and security updates, and the notice warned of possible "decreased performance with continued use" (Google Home Help). In plain terms, the heat still comes on, but the "smart" part is gone.

Most smart products never tell you how long support lasts

That kind of notice is the exception. In a November 2024 study, FTC staff reviewed 184 connected products and found nearly 89% did not disclose on their websites how long they would receive software updates. Basic internet searches turned up no support information at all for 124 of them (Federal Trade Commission). The FTC staff also noted that failing to disclose support duration can raise legal issues under the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act for products sold with written warranties (Federal Trade Commission).

Safety first: if your thermostat was linked to smoke or CO alarms to shut down the furnace or air handler during an alarm, find out whether that link still works. If support has ended, it may have stopped quietly. Your smoke and CO alarms still sound on their own, but the automatic HVAC shut-off may not happen.

Labels and laws are coming, slowly

  • United States: The FCC's voluntary U.S. Cyber Trust Mark will put a QR code on qualifying wireless smart products. The code will link to a registry showing details like the support period and whether security updates are automatic (FCC). As of October 2026, though, no product had been authorized to carry the label (Spilma).
  • European Union: The Cyber Resilience Act will require manufacturers to state the end date of a product's support period (month and year) at the time of purchase. It applies fully from December 11, 2027 (European Commission). U.S. buyers may benefit indirectly when global manufacturers publish one support date for every market.

Checklist: buying a thermostat that keeps working

Question to askWhy it matters
Does the manufacturer publish a support end date or a minimum support period?Without one, you can't know how long the app and security updates will last (Federal Trade Commission).
Does the schedule run on the device, not only in the cloud?A local schedule keeps running through internet outages and after support ends (Google Home Help).
Can it be controlled locally (open standard or local network)?Local control lessens the dependence on one company's servers. See our Matter thermostat guide.
Does it fully support your equipment's staging (heat pump, auxiliary heat, dual fuel)?Equipment compatibility matters more than app features for comfort and efficiency.
Are any safety interlocks or utility programs tied to the cloud?These are the first things to stop when support ends (Google Home Help).
Is the wiring standard and labeled (including a C-wire)?Standard, labeled wiring makes a future replacement quick and inexpensive, whatever brand you choose.

If your thermostat has already lost support

There's no emergency. Confirm the schedule and setpoints at the wall, check any alarm-to-HVAC links, and plan a replacement on your own timeline rather than during a no-heat call in January. When you replace it, the equipment-side wiring and staging setup matter most. A thermostat set up wrong for a heat pump can run electric backup heat far more than needed, so have the installer confirm the configuration.

Limitations

Support terms vary by manufacturer and model, and can change. The example above describes one manufacturer's published notice. Other products may keep more or fewer features after support ends. Always read the specific end-of-support notice for your device.
